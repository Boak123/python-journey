What the plan covers

What Our Pest Control Service Covers

A general pest control plan protects your home from the everyday invaders that show up across the seasons. Here is how we handle the most common ones.

General Household Insects

Spiders, silverfish, earwigs, and centipedes are some of the most common indoor pests, and most share a single weakness. They are usually hunting other insects or seeking damp, dark hiding spots. We treat the harborage areas where they shelter and cut down the smaller bugs they feed on, which removes their reason to stay. Taking away the food supply does more than chasing individual spiders ever will.

Seasonal Invaders That Push Inside

Stink bugs, box elder bugs, crickets, and ground beetles tend to crowd indoors when the weather shifts. They slip in through tiny gaps around windows, vents, and the foundation as they search for warmth or shelter. We treat the exterior to stop them at the wall and point out the openings letting them through. Sealing those gaps keeps the next wave outside.

Wasps and Stinging Insects

Wasps and hornets like to build nests under eaves, in soffits, and along rooflines where they go unnoticed until the nest is large. Knocking one down yourself often triggers a defensive swarm, which is how most stings happen. We remove nests safely and treat the spots that draw them, so the area stays clear. Your porch and yard become usable again.

The Protective Barrier Around Your Home

The heart of good pest control is the exterior barrier. We treat the foundation, entry points, eaves, and the ground line where pests cross from outside to inside. Stopping them at that boundary means far fewer ever reach your living space. This barrier is what makes a recurring plan so effective.

Before you reach for the can

Why DIY Sprays Often Make Things Worse

A can of spray from the store feels like a solution, but it usually treats the wrong target. Surface sprays kill the foragers you can see while the nest and breeding members stay safe in a wall void or in the soil. Within days, fresh pests replace the ones you hit.

Some products make the problem bigger. Many repellent sprays scatter a colony instead of ending it, splitting it into several smaller groups in new locations. What looked like one ant trail becomes three. A targeted, professional treatment works the opposite way, carrying the active ingredient back to the source so the whole population is affected.

Keep the pressure down

Simple Steps That Keep Pests Out Between Visits

You can make your home far less inviting with a few habits.

  • Seal gaps around pipes, cables, and the foundation, since openings the width of a pencil are enough for many pests.
  • Fix dripping faucets and clear standing water, because moisture draws nearly everything.
  • Store food in sealed containers and wipe up crumbs the same day.
  • Trim shrubs and branches back from the walls so pests lose their bridge to the house.
